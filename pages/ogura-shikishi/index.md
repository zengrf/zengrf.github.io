<h2>Project Overview</h2>
<p>I am gradually translating, annotating, and typesetting the poems of the Ogura Hyakunin Isshu (小倉百人一首). Each sheet combines calligraphy practice, historical research, and modern commentary aimed at students encountering classical Japanese literature for the first time.</p>
</div>

<div class="reveal">
<h2>Featured Drafts</h2>
<ol>
  <li><strong>Poem 17 — Ariwara no Narihira</strong>. Notes on seasonal imagery and the topology of pilgrimage routes between Kyoto and Nara.</li>
  <li><strong>Poem 43 — Fujiwara no Tadahira</strong>. Commentary focusing on political rhetoric and metaphors of courtly duty.</li>
  <li><strong>Poem 100 — Fujiwara no Sadayori</strong>. Comparative translation with attention to waka meter and modern Mandarin/English renderings.</li>
</ol>
<p>High-resolution scans and printable worksheets will appear here as the drafts are cleaned and typeset.</p>
</div>

<div class="reveal">
<h2>Workflow</h2>
<ul>
  <li>Digitize the original 色紙 (shikishi) cards and restore the gold-leaf texture.</li>
  <li>Provide a literal translation, followed by a cultural or mathematical footnote when a poem inspires a combinatorial interpretation.</li>
  <li>Include vocabulary lists for students studying classical Japanese (bungo) alongside Mandarin glosses.</li>
</ul>
</div>
